1. Executive Summary
Alfaaz is a self-guided, Flutter-based mobile application that helps individuals with speech difficulties — such as stuttering, articulation disorders, or post-injury speech recovery — practice independently at home, in Urdu and English, without depending on costly and geographically limited in-person speech therapy. The app was conceived from direct personal experience coordinating consistent speech practice for a family member, and is built for the higher-middle-class segment of Pakistani households who face real cost and access barriers to sustained clinical therapy.
2. Problem Statement
Speech difficulties affect people of all ages, but consistent professional speech therapy is expensive, clinic-bound, and concentrated in major cities. Families who cannot sustain weekly in-person sessions have no structured, affordable way to keep practicing at home. Existing global apps are English-only and priced in USD, making them impractical for most local users, while local attempts in this space have either stayed academic or failed to scale.
3. Target Audience (ICP)
Higher-middle-class households in Pakistan where a family member (child or adult) has a speech difficulty, and who:
Cannot afford or sustain weekly clinical speech therapy long-term
Own smartphones and are comfortable using mobile apps
Are bilingual (Urdu and English) and want practice content in their own language
Want a structured, trackable way to support the affected family member's progress

5. Proposed Solution & Unique Value Proposition
Alfaaz is a fully self-guided practice tool — all teaching content (pronunciation models, exercises) is delivered by the app itself, with a family member able to passively monitor progress through a separate caregiver view. There is no live therapist requirement in this version, keeping the app affordable and immediately usable.
What makes it unique:
Localized Urdu and English content — a gap even global market leaders do not fill
On-device text-to-speech pronunciation models — removes the need for expensive audio/video production
Caregiver dashboard — gives family members visibility without requiring active participation
Positioned as a practice and confidence-building tool, not a clinical replacement — avoiding regulatory ambiguity around medical claims

7. Technical Architecture
The application will be built in Flutter for cross-platform delivery (Android and iOS), with Supabase as the backend for authentication, database, and file storage.
Figure 1: High-level application architecture
Key Packages
flutter_tts — on-device text-to-speech for Urdu and English pronunciation playback
record + just_audio — user voice recording and playback for self-comparison
shared_preferences — local caching of streak data
fl_chart — progress visualization on the dashboard
supabase_flutter — authentication, database access, and file storage
8. Database Design (Supabase / PostgreSQL)
The schema is designed to support learner accounts, linked caregiver accounts, a shared practice content library, and per-session progress tracking.
Figure 2: Entity-Relationship Diagram — Supabase Schema
Table Summary
users — stores both learner and caregiver accounts, differentiated by role
caregiver_links — links a caregiver account to one or more learner accounts
practice_words — the shared content library of Urdu/English words and sentences
practice_sessions — records each practice attempt, including recording reference and self-rating
streaks — tracks current and longest daily practice streaks per user

10. Expected Impact & Benefits
Affordability — reduces dependence on costly, recurring clinic visits
Accessibility — practice available anytime, without travel or scheduling constraints
Localization — genuinely useful Urdu content, unlike existing English-first alternatives
Motivation — gamified streaks encourage sustained daily practice
Family involvement — caregivers stay informed without needing to be actively present
Self-reliance — builds users' confidence and independence in communication, supporting fuller participation in daily and social life
11. Conclusion
Alfaaz addresses a genuine, personally-understood problem with a scope that is realistic to build within the internship timeline. It combines a real market gap (local-language speech practice), a technically achievable architecture (Flutter + Supabase, no custom audio/video production required), and a differentiated value proposition (caregiver visibility, localization, self-reliance focus) that positions it well for both the internship evaluation and future development beyond it.
